This is a great framing — the "move the loop and intermediate state out of context, not just spawn more agents" point is the right core, and the two-layer `execute_code` / `delegate_task` boundary is exactly the guardrail people get wrong.

I've been benchmarking this fan-out-with-verification pattern on weak models (to stress where it breaks), and a few findings landed squarely on the **adversarial-convergence** section you flagged as the novel piece. Offering them here as additions to *your* skill — all four slot into your existing structure, none change the framing. **1. Execution beats refutation when a claim is testable** (the one I'd most want in).
Refutation is asymmetric — it can only *drop* claims, and a confident refuter *over-kills*: it marks a real, reachable bug `false_positive` on threat-model grounds ("that host-check substring is fine", "that null byte can't reach the sink") when the bug actually fires. A refute-only pipeline has no way to overrule a wrong refutation. Adding one ground-truth gate fixes it: for any claim a small script can exercise, fan out a cheap child to write the minimal repro, run it (Layer A `terminal`), and treat exit-0 as authoritative — a passing repro *resurrects* a claim the refuters killed. In a 12-finding run on a real codebase, burden-of-proof refutation dropped 10/10 false positives but also over-killed 2/2 *real* bugs; only running the repro brought them back. Net rule: **surface a claim if it survived refutation OR an execution repro confirms it.**

**2. Burden of proof in the refute round (tightens steps 3–4).** Default a claim to *false until proven*, not true until refuted — give each refuter the full file (not just the claim line), require it to name the specific guard to *kill* a claim and to trace attacker→source→sink to *keep* one. The reason: a weak finder + a weak refuter is a false-positive machine (cheap skeptics rubber-stamped ~75% of false positives in our runs), so "no refuter broke it" silently defaults claims to true.

**3. `when_not_to_use`: reasoning bottleneck ≠ coverage bottleneck.** Fan-out helps when the work *exceeds one pass* (coverage); it does **not** help a weak model *reason* better — splitting evidence across children makes multi-hop chaining worse, and if one focused pass already nails it, fan-out is a wash. (Measured: weak-model multi-hop QA didn't improve under fan-out; coverage sweeps over a 700k-token corpus went from ~0.02 to 1.0.)

**4. Empty children silently capping recall** (a Pitfall + a checklist line). Distinct from your "stalled child wrote anyway" note: a reasoning-model child can burn its whole token budget *thinking*, hit the limit, and return *empty* — its chunk drops from the union with no error and recall is quietly halved. Cheap idempotent fix: detect the missing/zero-length holes and re-dispatch only those.
